## Small induced batches have more usable host capacity than the common-palette test suggests

Two further reductions are now proved.

First, in a smallest counterexample, every vertex set of order at most n/10 is automatically host-colourable. Thus, at every sublinear batch scale, finding an affordable batch becomes a numerical induced-subgraph problem; no additional host-compatibility hypothesis is needed.

Second, even without that connectivity assumption, missing root incidences can pay for failed triangle hosts. A finite weighted argument shows that an affordable complete-root batch remains affordable under arbitrary chordal-compatible deletions of its root attachments, provided the retained root is sufficiently large relative to the batch’s clique number.

The second result extends your path-power batch to arbitrary chordal root-neighborhood patterns, and to nearby ratios. These results do not yet force an affordable batch or a terminal construction in every chordal graph.
